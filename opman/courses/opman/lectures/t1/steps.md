## What a process is | 4 min

:::idea The idea
A **process** is a set of **activities** that transforms **input** into **output**. Keep those three words in your head and make a sentence with them: that is the definition the teacher expects, in the tests and in the state exam.
:::

Two sentences frame the whole course:

- **Structure follows strategy** (Chandler, 1960s): you can design the operation properly only when the strategy of the organisation is clear.
- **Performance = strategy + operational efficiency** (Porter): you raise performance by selling more (strategy) and by running the operation efficiently. The operation always costs money, so its cost can never be zero: make it as cost-effective as possible.

### What "good" looks like: the five performance objectives

| Objective | The question | Measures from your iPhone 11 video |
|---|---|---|
| **Q**uality | Built right first time? | first-pass yield, rework rate |
| **S**peed | How fast does one unit go through? | cycle time, units per hour |
| **D**ependability | Delivered when planned? | schedule attainment, on-time release |
| **F**lexibility | Can it adapt without losing flow? | model and colour changeover |
| **C**ost | Are the resources used efficiently? | scrap + rework per unit, labour utilization |

## Classify your process | 6 min

:::teach Teacher said
The process typology has to be in your video. And: "every business student in the world should understand the difference" between piece, series and mass production. Remember the graph: **variety** against **volume**.
:::

### By production: variety against volume

- **Piece production**: one or very few of each product, but something different for every customer (a tailor, a jeweller).
- **Series production**: in the middle: a decent range of products, in good but not huge volume.
- **Mass production**: few products (5, 10 references) in a very big volume.

The book splits manufacturing into five: **project, jobbing, batch, mass, continuous**. A project gives one unique output; continuous gives one product in a huge volume (water, electricity, oil). Services go from **professional services** (consulting: every job unique) to **mass services** (a call centre: many people asking for the same thing).

:::ana Think of it like this
In a water company the operations manager thinks about pipes, valves and pressure. In a tailor's shop they think about one workstation with everything at hand. The type of process tells you what you will worry about.
:::

### The other ways to classify

| Classify by | Types | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Sales order | to **order** / to **stock** | a shop full of products waiting to be sold works to stock |
| Technology | **mechanical** / **chemical** / **biological or biochemical** | bending metal / a chemical reaction / yogurt, beer, wine |
| Layout | **individual** / **flow** / **group** (by product or by technology) / **phase** | a watchmaker / an assembly line / a kitchen with areas per technique / Airbus A380 |
| Location | **mobile** / **immobile** | a food truck, a building site / a factory |
| Continuity | **continuous** / **intermittent** | an airline call centre, a metal oven / a factory that stops at the weekend |

- Bending metal can be undone; a chemical reaction mostly can't.
- Group by **product** when you make few products; group by **technology** when you make many (all the drills together, all the wood work together).

### Worked example from the lecture: a screen factory

Series (several screen types, not one per customer) · to stock (made before anyone buys) · mechanical · flow, grouped by product · immobile · intermittent (it could be stopped, even if it runs 24/7).

## By time and by nature: where your process sits | 5 min

:::trap Exam trap
Many videos get this wrong. **By time** and **by nature** don't put your process in a box: they **place it in the chain**. Your process is the production stage and the core process; then name the main process before it, the main one after it, at least one support process and one service process.
:::

| | Wood-cutting line (lecture slide) |
|---|---|
| Pre-production | cutting the wood |
| Production | the line cuts wood blocks |
| Post-production | packaging |
| Core / main | the line cuts wood blocks |
| Support | cold saw maintenance |
| Service | office supplies purchasing |

- **Core**: what the customer pays for and thanks you for.
- **Support**: helps the core work (machine maintenance).
- **Service**: not your business, but you still manage it (the canteen, the coffee, gardening): bad food makes workers ill.

### Porter's value chain (1985)

| Core processes | Support processes |
|---|---|
| inbound logistics → operations → outbound logistics → marketing & sales → service | firm infrastructure · human resource management · technology development · procurement |

Together they create the **margin**.

:::skip Don't force your process into it
Porter described US companies of the 1980s (make first, then sell). Your process may work differently. What matters: some activities give value, others support the organisation to make a margin.
:::

## Portfolio, legal issues and location | 5 min

:::teach From the lecture (no slide in your folder)
This part is from Lecture 3 as it was spoken. Check it against your own Lecture 3 slides.
:::

- **Product portfolio** = the offer of the organisation (for the faculty: bachelor and master programmes, PhD, MBA…).
- **Product family**: a fridge and a washing machine look completely different to the customer, but in production they share parts: an electric motor, metal sheets in standard sizes (40, 60, 90, 120 cm). Like Lego: few pieces, many products. Car makers do the same with platforms.
- Why operations care: the customer sees 200 products, inside you combine a few parts. Analyse the portfolio to find where the customer sees the value.
- The **BCG matrix** (relative market share against market growth) shapes the operation: a **cash cow** (stable market, high share) needs a calm, stable operation; a **star** (growing market, high share) needs a fast, innovative one; low share in a market that doesn't grow → why keep making it?
- **Legal issues**: managers are legally responsible. A product needs its declaration of conformity and certification for the region, and labels in the local language (Czech-only labels can't be sold in Germany). IKEA solves it with picture-only manuals.

:::trap Not explained in your materials
Practice Test 2 asks for the difference between a *declaration of conformity* and *product certification*. Neither the slides nor the recordings you have explain it: look it up in your Lecture 3 slides.
:::

### Location: outsourcing and offshoring

- **Outsourcing**: another legal entity does the work for you (a commercial relationship). **Insourcing**: your own entity (your employees).
- **Offshoring**: a different geographical region, usually for cheaper labour.
- **Reshoring**: coming back, because logistics and coordination costs grow and wages abroad rise.
- From the paper: put **small factories next to the markets** instead of one huge plant far away.
