## Lean and the Toyota Production System | 6 min

:::idea The idea
**Lean** is a methodology that increases **value** to the customer by reducing **waste** in a **continuous improvement** environment. Craft production is flexible but expensive; mass production is cheap but offers little variety; lean gets both.
:::

The slide's toolbox: VSM, Mieruka, Jidoka, Poka Yoke, Kanban, TRIZ, DBR, TOC, SMED, TPM. This course explains Kanban, DBR, TOC and TPM.

## Lean vs Six Sigma, and TOC | 5 min

- **Lean**: effectiveness. It sets the standard (the average): 10 seconds per task.
- **Six Sigma**: **variability**. Without it the 10-second task takes anywhere from 2 to 20 seconds, and many outputs fall outside what the customer wants.

:::trap Exam trap
If you explain Six Sigma without the word **variability**, the answer is wrong.
:::

### Theory of Constraints (Eliyahu Goldratt, *The Goal*)

Basic concepts: **throughput** (how fast the system generates money), **inventory** (everything the system has to create throughput), **operational expenses** (the money needed to turn inventory into throughput).

## ABC-XYZ, pull, JIT, JIS, Kanban | 5 min

**ABC-XYZ** applies Pareto to the warehouse: **ABC** by value (high, medium, low), **XYZ** by dependability of use (regular to erratic). High-value items used regularly (AX) can flow just in time; low-value, erratic ones (CZ) stay in stock.

<div class="mg" data-g="sort" data-set="abc"></div>

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pull** | "Give me what I need in the right time." |
| **Just in time (JIT)** | Make only what you need, when you need it, in the amount needed. |
| **Just in sequence (JIS)** | Inventory matching JIT: a warehouse-free process delivering parts in line with the assembly sequence. |
| **Kanban** | Cards (signals) that control the flow: "take one, make one". |

## Support processes: facility management and TPM | 5 min

Porter's value chain again: the core processes, and the support and service processes around them.

- **Facility management** "is a profession that encompasses multiple disciplines to ensure functionality of the built environment by integrating people, place, process and technology" (IFMA). Lifts, heating, cleaning: not your business, but you must care. It has **11 core competences**, from communication and emergency preparedness to real estate and technology.
- **TPM, Total Productive Maintenance**: **Total**: every aspect, everyone from top management to the workers. **Productive**: maintenance without stopping production. **Maintenance**: keeping the equipment in good condition. The goal: **no product defects, no unplanned downtime, no accidents**.

TPM has 6 elements: teamwork towards common goals · improve equipment efficiency by attacking the big losses · involve workers in daily maintenance · make maintenance efficient and effective · machine life cycle and design · training for everyone. The OEE losses are exactly what TPM removes.
