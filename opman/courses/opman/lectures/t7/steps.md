## Four industrial revolutions | 3 min

<div class="mg" data-g="order" data-set="rev"></div>

| | Year | The new element |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | 1784, UK | **steam**: you boil water and can move machines |
| 2nd | 1870 | **electricity**: stable energy you plug into a machine |
| 3rd | 1969 | **electronics**: the first microchip, computers and automation |
| 4th | 2012, Germany | **connectivity**: the Internet of Things applied to industry (IIoT) |

## The 17 technologies | 8 min

The test asks: "Explain and give one example in manufacturing of the technology …" for each one. The descriptions come from the seminar slides; the examples come from **your Team O workshop sheet**.

| # | Technology | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Advanced robotics** | Robots capable of co-operating with humans by means of sensors; more flexibility, variability, complexity and speed. | BMW Group uses NVIDIA's Isaac robotics platform to run customised cars, several models on one line, just in time and just in sequence. |
| 2 | **Big data** | Processing large data for optimization and improvement … real-time management. | Dolle A/S (Denmark) combined shop-floor data with ERP data for descriptive and predictive analytics. |
| 3 | **Digital modelling** | Simulation models of digital factories; optimize production processes with virtual reality. | BMW simulates its factories in NVIDIA Omniverse: expected efficiency gains of up to 30 %. |
| 4 | **Internet of Things** | Connectivity and interaction between objects, and remote control. | Bosch's plant in Blaichach uses over 60 000 sensors for monitoring and predictive maintenance. |
| 5 | **Human-machine interaction** | Collaboration between robots and people based on voice recognition and gestures. | Workers use Siemens SIMATIC HMI touchscreens to control and monitor machines. |
| 6 | **Augmented reality** | Integrates artificially created objects into an image of the real world. | Microsoft HoloLens shows 3D instructions to guide workers during industrial tasks. |
| 7 | **Virtual assistants** | Recognise situations derived from individual user needs. | Siemens Industrial Copilot helps factory workers find and solve machine problems. |
| 8 | **Energy sourcing** | Energy from ambient temperature, vibration or airflow. | BMW uses four wind turbines at its Leipzig factory. |
| 9 | **New sensors** | Data collection and analysis in various areas, with wireless transmission. | ABB Ability: wireless sensors on factory motors. |
| 10 | **Machine learning** | The ability to adapt to and react positively to changes in the environment. | Google DeepMind's AI cuts data-centre cooling energy. |
| 11 | **Biosensors** | Useful plants (chlorophyll) as sensors for monitoring indoor environmental parameters. | Vivent PhytlSigns: plants used as sensors in greenhouses. |
| 12 | **Terahertz** | Fast wireless data transfer, up to 10^11 bits per second. | TeraView TeraCota checks car paint thickness on the line. |
| 13 | **Autonomous robots** | Programmed robots, totally autonomous from human management and decision-making. | Symbotic robots in warehousing. |
| 14 | **Brain interface** | Lets brain activity trigger external activity. | Non-invasive wearable EEG (Fraunhofer). |
| 15 | **Cognitive computing** | Signal processing and learning by example, associative memory, AI and network decision making. | OpenText Magellan cognitive analytics. |
| 16 | **Quantum technologies** | Extremely high data transfer speeds with high security. | SavantX HONE: quantum-powered container logistics. |
| 17 | **New graphene characteristics** | The strongest material; conducts heat and electricity; almost transparent. | Graphene-enhanced smart packaging (University of Manchester). |

:::ana Learn them in pairs
Advanced robotics (co-operate with humans) vs autonomous robots (no humans). Augmented reality (objects in the real image) vs digital modelling (a digital factory). Biosensors (plants) vs new sensors (wireless data). Terahertz (fast transfer) vs quantum (fast and secure).
:::
