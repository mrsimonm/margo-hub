Market Equilibrium in the Perfectly
Competitive Market
• A market situation when consumers are willing to buy
and suppliers are willing to sell a certain quantity of
goods or services at the price acceptable for both parties
(equilibrium price).
• The price is a compromise between the opposite
interests of both sides concerning the price.
• Graphically the market E is depicted as the intersection
of the supply curve and the demand curve.
• This point is called equilibrium point.
=== SLIDE 20 ===
20
Market Equilibrium on the Perfectly
Competitive Markets – cont.
• In the perfectly competitive market, the interest of sellers
to set higher than equilibrium prices results in the surplus
of goods/services, which leads to lowering of the price.
• The interest of buyers to buy at a lower prices than
equilibrium price will result in the shortage of
goods/services and this will lead to price increases.
• Shifts of one or both curves shift the equilibrium point.
• The area where the new E point will be located is given.
