Definition of Economics
Economics is the study of:
• how societies with limited, scarce resources
decide what gets produced, how, and for
whom
• how individuals and societies make choices
under scarcity and the implications of those
choices
=== SLIDE 8 ===
8
Scarcity vs. Unlimited Wants
• Scarcity implies the need for a choice
among alternatives– no free lunch.
• Trade Off - when you choose option A over
option B; you sacrifice of B
• Wants are unlimited while resources for
satisfying them are finite.
• A need is something that you have to have.
• A want (desire) is something you would like
to have.
=== SLIDE 9 ===
9
Opportunity cost
• Definition: The value of the next-best
alternative that must be forgone in
order to undertake an activity.
• The opportunity cost of any good or
services is the amount of other goods
or services that must be given up to
obtain it.
=== SLIDE 10 ===

Microeconomics vs. Macroeconomics
• Microeconomics focuses on how
decisions are made by individuals and
firms and the consequences of those
decisions.
• Macroeconomics aggregates for
millions of actors (consumers, firms, the
whole economy) – aggregate level

The Assumption of Rationality
Economists assume that people are rational.
Rationality = try to fulfill their goals as best they
can with available resources and information.
A rational approach
= compare benefits vs. costs
=== SLIDE 18 ===

Homo Economicus, or the Economic
Human
• Humans are rational and narrowly self-
interested actors who have the ability to make
judgments about their subjectively defined
goals.
• Using these rational assessments, homo
economicus attempts to maximize utility as a
consumer and economic profit as a producer.

Short Run vs. Long Run
• Short run: A period of time during which some
of a firm’s inputs cannot be varied or in which a
firm or household has not fully adapted to a
price change.
• Long run: A period of time long enough for a
firm to be able to vary all its factors of
productions or for a firm or househod to adapt
fully to a price change.
