1. Firm‘s Analysis - Introduction
Assumptions:
– The market of goods (Supply → firms; benefit of teamwork; reduction of transaction costs)
– A firm is an association of individuals who have
organized themselves for the purpose of turning inputs into outputs
– the firm as a single decision-making unit
Firm’s goal:
– to maximize profit and minimize costs
=== SLIDE 7 ===

Behavior of Profit-Maximizing Firms
Basic decisions:
1. How much output to supply (QE)?
2. Which production technology to use?
3. How much of each input to demand?
• Firm‘s behavior depends on the competitive
environment in which it operates.

Basic rule: Maximization of profit vs. Minimization of costs
Revenue (total revenue, TR) – how much we get from selling our
product?
TR = P x Q
Costs (total costs, TC): Explicit (accounting) costs – actually spent
money + Implicit (opportunity costs)
Profit – economic profit vs. accounting profit:
Economic profit (EP) = accounting profit (AP) - opportunity costs
(OC, implicit costs)
EP (π) = AP – OC; AP = revenues (TR) – explicit costs
=== SLIDE 9 ===

Fashion designer‘s butigue example
Jane is a fashion designer. She works herself and uses a
special sewing machine of which is the owner.
• She makes and sells usually three tunics per week.
• The price of tunic is CZK 3,500.
• She hires a shop, where is her boutique and pays CZK
15,000 per month. Cost of material is usually CZK 800 per
one tunic.
• She is an entrepreneur. At the same time, this designer
can work for nearby House of Fashion and be an
employee with the remuneration CZK 35,000 per month.
=== SLIDE 10 ===
10
Fashion designer‘s butigue example - cont.
1. Let us calculate the accounting profit of this designer.
TR = P*Q
TR = (3,500*3)*4 = 42,000 per month
Explicit costs = FC + VC = 15,000 + 800 * (3*4) = 15,000 + 9,600 =
24,600
Accounting profit = 42,000 – 24,600 = 17,400
2. Now we calculate economic profit.
EP = AP - implicit cost
EP = 17,400 - 35,000 = -17,600
For this designer is better to stop her business and start to be an
employee.

1. Production Function and Cost
Curves in the Short Run
Short-Run vs. Long-Run Decisions
• The short run is a period of time for which two
conditions hold:
1. The firm is operating under a fixed scale (fixed
factor) of production, and
2. Firms can neither enter nor exit an industry.
• The long run is a period of time for which there are no
fixed factors of production. Firms can increase or
decrease scale of operation, and new firms can enter
and existing firms can exit the industry.

Production Function - Total Physical
Product
• Definition: Production function gives the
maximum amount of output than can be
produced using any specified amount of inputs.
• The production function or total product
function is a numerical or mathematical
expression of a relationship between inputs and
outputs. It shows units of total product as a
function of units of inputs.
Q = f(F1, F2, F3, …… Fn)
Q = TPP

Diminishing Marginal Returns (DMR)
The Low of Diminishing Marginal Returns states that:
• When additional units of a variable input are added
to fixed inputs, the marginal product of the variable
input declines.
• When there is at least one fixed input, eventually a
point is reached at which the marginal product of an
additional worker begins to fall.

Standard Shape of the Production Function
￼
• Q = f(K, L); K is constant
• The law of diminishing
marginal returns operates
from point A
• MPP ↑ → TPP grows
more quickly than the input
• MPP ↓ → TPP grows
more slowly than the input
• MPP = 0 → TPP is at the
maximum
• MPP < 0 → TPP decreases
• MPP > APP ↑
MPP < APP ↓
MPP = APP → APP = max
