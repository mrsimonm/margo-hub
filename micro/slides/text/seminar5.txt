Solution
• Use example:
1.The price of blueberries is EUR 6 per half of the litre. This is
too expansive for Jane, and she doesn‘t purchase anything.
•When price decreases to P = 5, she purchases half of the litre.
•Per price four she is willing to buy one litre.
•Per price 3, Jane purchases two litres blueberries.
•Per price two she buys two and half of litre.
2.Therefore, we can summarize Jan‘s total utility for her
consumption: TU = 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 = 14.
3.The actual market price is € 2,- and Jane purchases 2,5 litres
of blueberries. She spends 5 EUR for blueberries e.g.
2*(2,5*2) = 10. Price is per half of the litre.
4.Consumer surplus = TU – real expenditure = 14 -10 = 4

Basic rule: Maximization of profit vs. Minimization of costs
Revenue (total revenue, TR) – how much we get from selling our
product?
TR = P x Q
Costs (total costs, TC): Explicit (accounting) costs– actually spent
money + Implicit (opportunity costs)
Profit– economic profit vs. accounting profit:
Economic profit (EP) = accounting profit (AP) - opportunity costs
(OC, implicit costs)
EP (π) = AP – OC; AP = revenues (TR) – explicit costs
