Indifference Curves Cannot Intersect
• If indifference curves crossed, such as point A, then A, B and C

=== SLIDE 9 ===
9
Characteristics of Indifference Curves
• There are the indifference curves at every point
of the graph (there is an infinite amount of IC);
• Indifference curves are downward (consider
desirable goods);
• The more IC are distant from the outset (origin),
the greater the benefit;
• Indifference curves cannot intersect;
• Indifference curves are convex to the origin - the
law of diminishing marginal utility applies;

Categories of Demand Elasticity
￼
￼
￼
• │EPD│= 1 Unitary elasticity; the percentage change in quantity
demanded equals the percentage change in price; Total Revenue
(TR) does‘t change
• │EPD│ < 1 Demand is inelastic; the percentage change in
quantity demanded is less than the percentage change
in price; TR increases
• │EPD│ > 1 Demand is elastic, the percentage change in quantity
demanded changes by greater percentage than does
the price; TR decreases.
• EPD = 0 Perfectly inelastic demand
• EPD = ∞ Perfectly elastic demand
