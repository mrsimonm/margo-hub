=== SLIDE 21 ===

Indifference Analysis
• Indifference curve represents a set of
possible consumption bundles between
which the individual is indifferent
- shows all combinations of two goods
that provide the consumer with the
same total satisfaction or total utility
(IC)
• Indifference map - a collection of
(selected) indifference curves
=== SLIDE 22 ===
22
Indifference Curves Cannot Intersect
• If indifference curves crossed, such as point A, then A, B and C
will have the same utility.
• But point B is a combination with same salami and more
cheese than point C must represent a higher level of utility.
￼
=== SLIDE 23 ===
23
Characteristics of Indifference Curves
• There are the indifference curves at every point
of the graph (there is an infinite amount of IC);
• Indifference curves are downward (consider
desirable goods);
• The more IC are distant from the outset (origin),
the greater the benefit;
• Indifference curves cannot intersect;
• Indifference curves are convex to the origin - the
law of diminishing marginal utility applies;

Marginal Rate of Substitution
• Marginal rate of substitution
(MRSC) - maximum amount of a
good that a consumer is willing to
give up in order to obtain one
additional unit of another good.
MRSC = ΔY/ Δ X = MUX / MUY
(TU is constant)
• MRSC = the slope of
indifference curve (use absolute
value)
